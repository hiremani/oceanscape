# Automotive UX guidance for a driver-facing center-screen 3D HUD and car-controls app (Fisker Ocean, 17.1-inch screen, optional comma device)

Research date: 2026-11-01. Sources are dated inline where it matters. Items marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be confirmed from a primary source. Standards texts (ISO 15008 clause values, SAE J2364/J2365 current text, NHTSA DOT HS 812 360) are paywalled or blocked and are recorded as gaps rather than guessed.

A note on the brief: the brief says the Ocean runs "landscape in drive" and "can rotate to portrait when parked". Every source found says the opposite for the stock car: Control Mode is the portrait layout used while driving and Hollywood Mode is the 16:9 landscape layout available only in Park (see Q3). Worth checking which orientation Pulse actually drives the panel in before committing to a layout.

## Q1. What do NHTSA, ISO 15008, SAE J2364/J2365, Euro NCAP 2026 and the Alliance (AAM) guidelines prescribe numerically?

### Takeaway
The numbers that recur across every guideline are: no single off-road glance over 2.0 s, no more than 12 s total eyes-off-road (NHTSA) or 20 s (Alliance) per task, 15 s static task time (SAE J2364), a 0.25 s device response best practice, a 30-degree downward viewing-angle cone, one-handed operation, and a "per se lock-out while moving" list (video, manual text entry, scrolling text, long reading, non-driving images). Euro NCAP 2026 adds its own: distraction is a 3-4 s single glance or 10 s cumulative in 30 s, essential driving information (speed, speed limit, ADAS state, lights) must sit within a 30 degree line-of-sight cone, touch targets must be at least 10 x 10 mm with 4 mm gaps, and menu depth is capped at 2 steps.

### Cited Findings

**NHTSA Phase 1 Visual-Manual Driver Distraction Guidelines (Federal Register, 26 Apr 2013, voluntary, for OE in-vehicle devices)**
- Eye-glance acceptance test (24 participants, each task done once while car-following at 50 mph): a task should be locked out while driving unless, for at least 21 of 24 participants, (a) no more than 15% of glances away from the road exceed 2.0 s, (b) the mean glance duration is at or below 2.0 s, and (c) the sum of off-road glance durations is at or below 12.0 s — [NHTSA Guidelines, Federal Register 78 FR 24818, sec. VI](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Occlusion alternative: 1.5 s viewing / 1.5 s occluded intervals per ISO 16673:2007; the task passes if mean Total Shutter Open Time (TSOT) is 12.0 s or less for at least 21 of 24 participants (the criterion was raised from the 9 s originally proposed) — [NHTSA Guidelines](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- The 12 s figure is the 85th-percentile total eyes-off-road time for manual radio tuning in NHTSA's test-track study (initially 12.1 s, rounded to a multiple of 1.5 s for occlusion compatibility); the Alliance had argued for 20 s — [NHTSA DOT HS 811 781](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/811781.pdf); [GardnerWeb](https://www.gardnerweb.com/news/carmakers-seek-a-delay-on-distraction-guidelines)
- "Driving" is defined as: transmission not in Park (for vehicles with a Park position), or, for vehicles without one, whenever the vehicle is not stopped with the engine off; commenters argued for "stopped but not in Park" exceptions and NHTSA kept Park as the line — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. IV.C and Definitions](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Per se lock-outs (always inaccessible to the driver while driving, and inaccessible to a passenger if the display is in the driver's view): (1) functions not intended for use while driving; (2) manual text entry for messaging, communication or browsing; (3) displaying video (exceptions: FMVSS-required views; the rear-view image while in reverse, which must end at 10 mph, 10 m or 10 s after leaving reverse); (4) displaying non-video graphical or photographic images (exceptions: maps and driving-related images; static images that help a selection in a non-driving task such as album art, if they auto-extinguish when the task completes; standardized symbols and trademarks); (5) automatically scrolling text (horizontal or vertical, moving at a pace not controlled by the driver); (6) displaying text to be read from books, periodicals, web pages, social media, advertising, or text messages — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. V.F](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Map detail: "the display of informational detail not critical to navigation, such as photorealistic images, satellite images, or three-dimensional images is not recommended" — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. V.F.3.c](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- The original proposal locked out reading more than 30 characters per task and manual text entry of more than six button presses; in the final version the 30-character reading cap was dropped in favor of the acceptance test, because 30 characters was derived from Japanese (JAMA) guidance and English needs roughly 93-133 characters for the same content — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. IV.E.2](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Display placement: the downward viewing angle at the geometric center of each active display should be below the 2D or 3D maximum (formula based on nominal eye height; for eye heights at or below 1146.2 mm the 2D maximum is 30 degrees); no part of the device may obstruct the view of the road or of required controls — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. V.C](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Single-handed operation: at most one hand required for any input while moving; no simultaneous two-hand input; reach through steering wheel openings not required — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. V.I](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Interruptibility: no uninterruptible sequences; a driver should be able to resume where they left off; the device may time out to a default state only for low-priority states — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. V.J](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Device response time: feedback should be "timely and clearly perceptible"; best practice maximum 0.25 s; if response exceeds 2.00 s a clearly perceptible "working" indication must be shown — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. V.K](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Sound: devices should not mask warnings from inside or outside the vehicle and must be mutable — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. V.H](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Dynamic non-safety information (e.g. animations) must be hideable by dimming, blanking, changing state or repositioning so it "cannot be seen by a driver while driving"; devices should clearly distinguish functions meant for use while driving from those that are not — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. V.L and V.M](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Status: the guidelines are voluntary; Phase 2 (portable and aftermarket devices, which is the category this app falls into) was proposed in 2016 and its final status was not confirmed — [SWOV summary of Phase 2](https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/visual-manual-nhtsa-driver-distraction-guidelines-portable-and-aftermarket-devices); [National Academies 2022 review](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/26550/chapter/4)

**Alliance of Automobile Manufacturers "Driver Focus-Telematics" guidelines (2006 edition, as characterized by NHTSA)**
- Principle 2.1A ("2/20 rule"): individual glances to the device should not exceed 2.0 s and total glance time per task should not exceed 20.0 s, benchmarked on 85th-percentile radio tuning; Principle 2.1B instead uses lane exceedances and headway variability relative to a reference task — [NHTSA DOT HS 811 781](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/811781.pdf); [Garrott, SAE Jan 2012](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/documents/jan_2012_sae_garrott.pdf)
- NHTSA judged the Alliance's 85th-percentile estimate flawed (multiplying 85th-percentile glance duration by 85th-percentile glance count overstates) and settled on 12 s — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. IV.G](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)

**SAE J2364 (15-second rule) and J2365 (task-time estimation)**
- J2364: navigation tasks involving visual displays and manual controls should not be allowed in a moving vehicle if they take more than 15 s measured statically (parked); the author notes "a strong case exists for a lower limit of about 10 seconds" and that most destination entry will fail — [Green, "The 15-Second Rule for Driver Information Systems", ITS America 1999 (TRID)](https://trid.trb.org/View/511388)
- J2365 estimates task time by summing times for task elements (decide, press button, etc.); the 2002 version is withdrawn and the current J2365_201607 merges the calculation and the J2364 measurement procedure into one document — [SAE J2365_201607](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/J2365_201607); [SAE J2365_200205 (withdrawn)](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/J2365_200205)

**ISO 15008:2017 (legibility of in-vehicle displays)**
- Scope: minimum image-quality and legibility requirements for displays showing dynamic information to the driver of a passenger car while moving; clause 4.3.2 covers minimum contrast ratio, 4.5.2 character height; contrast is measured per SAE J1757/1 at 18-28 C; sunlight contrast need only be met along the "critical specular line"; standard default angles 45/20 degrees are accepted for sunlight measurement — [ISO 15008:2017 preview, iTeh](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/62784/ac1daa21f49545c69dc9a5b33259a059/ISO-15008-2017.pdf)
- Character height is specified as an angular dimension in arcmin (1 arcmin = 1/60 degree); the standard also sets pixel-matrix minimums such as 5 x 7 for uppercase-only alphanumerics — [iTeh catalogue entry](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/d07adfe7-f551-4557-bb09-7236d8491cf6/iso-15008-2017)
- Secondary figures (not the standard's own text): a literature review reports Chengalur et al. (2004) recommending 20-22 arcmin character height with a minimum of 14, Stevens et al. (2002) 15 arcmin static and 24 arcmin moving, and states that guidelines above 24 arcmin are compliant with ISO 15008 — [Assessment of Automotive Visual Display Guidelines (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233611829_Assessment_of_Automotive_Visual_Display_Guidelines_and_Principles_A_Literature_Review)
- A revision is in progress (CD approved for DIS registration, June 2026) — [Albanian standards body listing](https://dps.gov.al/en/project/show/iso:proj:94756)

**Euro NCAP 2026 Safe Driving / Driver Engagement protocol v1.2 (July 2026, implementation January 2026)**
- Driver Engagement is 30 points: Driver Monitoring 25 (transient states 15, non-transient 10) and General Vehicle Controls 5 (Driving 2.5, Comfort & Infotainment 2.5) — [Euro NCAP Driver Engagement v1.2](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- Distraction definitions: Long Distraction = a single gaze away from the forward road of 3-4 s (preceded by a 4 s on-road gaze); Short Distraction / VATS = cumulative 10 s off-road within a 30 s window; the "in-vehicle infotainment system" is an explicit non-driving-task gaze target; the instrument cluster counts as a driving-task target — [Euro NCAP Driver Engagement v1.2, sec. 1.2](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- Microsleep = 1-2 s eye closure; sleep = 3 s or more; unresponsive = gaze does not return within 3 s of a distraction warning, or eyes closed 6 s or more — [same, sec. 1.3](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- Warning requirements: at 20 km/h or more a visual plus haptic and/or audible warning must be issued immediately on distraction; microsleep and sleep warnings must be distinct and more urgent than distraction warnings; FCW/AEB timing must shift at least 200 ms earlier for a distracted driver, activated within 1 s of a continuous off-road gaze; an Emergency Function must engage Assisted Mode no later than 5 s after the distinct warning phase starts — [same, sec. 1.4](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- "Inform if not functional": if DSM is compromised (sunglasses, hats) the driver must be told visually and/or audibly within 10 s; the visual notice must persist or repeat every 10 minutes while non-functional — [same, sec. 1.1.1.2](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- HMI definitions: "Direct physical input" = a control with mechanical movement findable by touch; "Direct touch input" = always-accessible touch control "e.g. a fixed bar with digital controls at the bottom of an in-vehicle infotainment screen"; "Menu-based touch input" = at most 2 steps counted from the home screen; "Home screen" = the screen used while driving, not a welcome screen — [same, Definitions](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- Essential driving information ("vehicle speed, applicable speed limit, ADAS status, and all exterior light status") must be in the "direct driver's line of sight": within +/-30 degrees laterally of the ocular reference point and above a plane 30 degrees below it; a control hidden by the steering wheel is out of the cone — [same, Definitions and sec. 2.1.1.3](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- Touch targets: minimum 10 x 10 mm with 4 mm separation (separation can relax if targets are larger); steering-wheel touch areas must be tactilely identifiable — [same, sec. 2.1.1.2](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- Direct physical input is required for direction indicators, gear selector, hazard lights, e-Call, horn, front and rear wipers; demist/defrost and mirrors may be direct touch; rear demist, wiper auto-sensitivity and comfort functions may be menu-based (max 2 steps); voice input needs a dedicated button and a non-voice alternative — [same, sec. 2.2](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- Gate: a car offering assisted driving must score at least 50% in both Driving Collaboration and Driver Monitoring "to prevent overreliance"; the Assisted Driving grade balances Vehicle Assistance against Driver Engagement — [same, sec. 1.1](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf); [AB Dynamics on Euro NCAP 2026](https://www.abdynamics.com/?p=16328); [EE Times Europe](https://www.eetimes.eu/?p=39718)

**Color conventions**
- ISO 2575:2021 "indicates the colours of possible optical tell-tales, which inform the driver of either correct operation or malfunctioning"; UN R121 allows ISO 2575 color coding where red, yellow and green convey failure/active status modes — [ISO 2575:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/68409.html); [UN R121 texts (globalautoregs)](https://globalautoregs.com/rules/132?show=texts)
- FMVSS 101 Table 2 allows green or blue for high beam; blue was the original choice to harmonize with ISO/ECE — [NHTSA interpretation 86-19](https://www.nhtsa.gov/interpretations/86-19)
- FHWA guidance: when critical information is color coded it "should be redundant with other coding (e.g., shape coding)" — [FHWA 98-057 ch. 3](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/98057/ch03a.cfm)

### Inferences
- For an aftermarket page on the center screen, NHTSA Phase 1 is the closest numeric yardstick even though Phase 2 is the formal category; the per se lock-out list maps directly onto what to gate behind "Park": video, free text entry, scrolling lists that move on their own, and long text.
- Euro NCAP's 30-degree cone and "fixed bar at the bottom of the infotainment screen" definition read like a spec for a persistent status strip (speed, limit, ADAS state, lights) at the top edge nearest the driver's sightline, plus a fixed control bar.
- Euro NCAP's 10 x 10 mm target and 2-step menu depth, plus NHTSA's 0.25 s response, are testable acceptance criteria for the car-controls catalogue.

### Gaps
- Exact ISO 15008 clause values (minimum contrast ratio, arcmin character height) could not be read; the standard is paywalled and the preview stops before clause 4.3.2.
- Current SAE J2364_201506 and J2365_201607 texts are paywalled; only the 1999 description of the 15 s rule was readable.
- NHTSA DOT HS 812 360 (2016 DVI human factors guidance, with color/flash/auditory numbers) returned HTTP 403 and could not be read.
- The Euro NCAP Assisted Driving v1.2 protocol (system status display criteria) was not read; only the Driver Engagement protocol was extracted.
- The primary Alliance 2006 document was not found online; its numbers are taken from NHTSA's characterization.
- No guideline found gives an explicit animation frame-rate or motion limit; NHTSA only requires that dynamic non-safety information be hideable and that scrolling not be automatic.

## Q2. How do Tesla, Rivian, Polestar/Volvo, Lucid, Hyundai/Kia and Mercedes lay out the center screen while driving, and what is locked while moving?

### Takeaway
Two patterns dominate: (a) a split landscape screen with a driver-side "car status" visualization and a passenger-side map, with a fixed bottom taskbar (Tesla, Rivian, Lucid's wide cockpit); (b) a card/tile home screen with a persistent climate bar at the bottom and status at the top (Polestar/Volvo Android Automotive, Mercedes Zero Layer, Hyundai ccNC). Every maker locks video and games in motion, and the loudest criticism is of anything that makes the driver look at the center screen for speed or basic controls (Volvo EX30, screen-only vents).

## Q4. What do openpilot, sunnypilot and FrogPilot show on the device, how is the alert hierarchy built, and what customizations do owners ask for?

### Takeaway
openpilot's alert model is a six-level priority enum crossed with three statuses (normal, userPrompt, critical), four sizes (none, small, mid, full), a visual-alert flag for the car's HUD (fcw, steerRequired, ldw) and a set of chimes; the same event escalates through status/size/sound in fixed steps (e.g. "Pay Attention" small/normal, then mid/userPrompt with chime, then full/critical "DISENGAGE IMMEDIATELY"). The forks add what owners ask for: path/lane width and colors keyed to driving mode, lead-chevron metrics, blind-spot indicators, green-light and lead-departure chimes, road name, standstill timer, speed-limit display, onroad brightness and screen-off/standby, and the ability to hide elements or the speedometer.

## Q5. What research exists on 3D "world view" visualizations vs 2D/abstract ones for driver trust and distraction?

### Takeaway
The evidence says what is shown matters more than how it is rendered: showing perception (detected objects, hazards) raises trust and situation awareness, showing predictions (other agents' future paths) adds workload and is rated worse, and 3D/lightfield display technology produced "almost no significant differences" versus a flat display. Several studies warn that richer visualizations can produce overtrust and that more simultaneous interfaces raise cognitive load under stress.

### Cited Findings
- Colley et al., MUM 2023, within-subject N=15: compared a baseline 2D rendering of automated-vehicle perception/prediction/planning with the same scene on a LumePad lightfield tablet and a LookingGlass holographic display; "we found almost no significant differences, thus, indicating that the display technology is less relevant for conveying automated functionality"; the only significant effect was in assessing whether the AV recognized other vehicles; Bayes factors gave moderate evidence of no difference for trust and perceived driving capability; the authors frame fancy displays as a possible "Halo Effect" risk (trust from "shiny" effects) — [Colley et al. 2023 (Ulm)](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.100/1-hci/hci-paper/2023/MUM_2023_3D_Display_final_compressed.pdf)
- Colley et al., IMWUT 2022, online between-subjects N=216, simulated AR windshield over real video: visualizing Situation Detection, Situation Prediction and Maneuver Planning in combinations; "Situation Prediction-related visualizations were perceived as worse than the remaining levels"; prediction raised mental workload (M 12.40 vs 9.89 on raw NASA-TLX, p=0.001); no significant effect on trust or understandability across conditions; "the results indicate the presence of overtrust in AVs" — [Colley et al. 2022 (Ulm)](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.100/institut/Papers/Prof_Rukzio/2022/IMWUT2022_functional_hierarchy_final.pdf)
- Prior results summarized in the Ulm papers: Lindemann et al. found an AR HUD highlighting hazards gave higher situation awareness than speed-and-navigation only, in low and high visibility; Häuslschmid et al. found a "world in miniature" view of the car's situation interpretation produced the greatest trust increase, though participants disagreed on whether it was necessary; Koo et al. found "why" + "how" explanations maximized trust but could overload; Colley et al. earlier found "no futuristic visualization is necessary", current HUDs can convey the needed information; a tablet version of pedestrian-intention display was rated significantly higher in cognitive load than an AR version — [Colley et al. 2023, Related Work](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.100/1-hci/hci-paper/2023/MUM_2023_3D_Display_final_compressed.pdf)
- STADT:up (German national project, Jan 2023-mid 2026, EUR 62.2M; Ergosign report 29 Jul 2026): a 30-participant Berlin study plus a VR simulator study (Jan 2026, four-minute continuous urban drives with a secondary task) found that "when the vehicle showed in real time what it perceived and how it assessed the situation, trust rose measurably"; stress rose when complex traffic coincided with distraction; on-screen text plus simultaneous voice forced cross-referencing and distracted; "more simultaneously active interfaces increased cognitive load measurably"; recommendation: show information when needed and hold it back when disruptive, adaptive and personalizable rather than static; their engineering UI reduced event flags from six categories to three (red, yellow, white) — [Ergosign STADT:up conclusion](https://www.ergosign.de/en/insights/blog/stadt-up-abschluss)
- Koegel et al., IEEE IV 2021 ("Thinking Wave"): proposes real-time visualizations of system effort and confidence ("Tidal" and "Tandem" concepts) to calibrate trust; prototypes only, no user study reported — [arXiv 2107.01763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01763)
- Production evidence on clutter: Rivian's surrounding-traffic visualization is "useful during assisted highway driving but busy around town" — [InsideEVs Rivian review](https://insideevs.com/reviews/735802/rivian-r1s-infotainment-review-2024/)
- NHTSA's own stance on map rendering: photorealistic, satellite and three-dimensional map detail "not critical to navigation... is not recommended" — [NHTSA Guidelines](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)

### Inferences
- A 3D world view earns its place by showing detections (lead, adjacent vehicles, lane the car is in, hazards), not by drawing predicted trajectories of other agents, which the strongest study rated worst and found most loading.
- Rendering fidelity (3D models, lighting) is unlikely to change trust or awareness; it mainly costs frame budget and risks overtrust. An abstract, low-detail 3D scene is defensible.
- Context-adaptive density (richer on highway with the comma engaged, sparser in town or at low speed, minimal at night) is the pattern the research supports; this matches Rivian's reviewer comment and STADT:up's recommendation.

### Gaps
- No study found that directly compares a perspective 3D "world view" against a 2D top-down or icon-only presentation on a center screen for distraction (glance time) in manual or L2 driving; the Ulm work is about highly automated vehicles and display technology.
- Effect sizes for Häuslschmid, Lindemann and Koo were not read from the original papers; they are cited as summarized in the Ulm related-work section.
- No research found on the Fisker-sized (17-inch) center screen specifically.

## Q6. Recognized patterns for safety-critical visualization: object/lane rendering, confidence, escalation, night modes, hierarchy

### Takeaway
Across production cars and openpilot forks the same conventions recur: proximity and risk by a white-to-red ramp, path/lane colored by control state, lead vehicle as a marked object with optional distance/time, a three-tier alert ladder with sound only at the upper tiers, a fixed status strip in the driver's sightline, and a dark/standby mode that wakes for alerts.

### Cited Findings
- Proximity ramp: Tesla's proximity lines "white, yellow, orange, or red... with white being the farthest and red being very close" — [Tesla Model Y manual (tesla.cn)](https://tesla.cn/ownersmanual/modely/en_us/GUID-80B80D48-E3A9-4857-864B-F4CC9B56FD7E.html); Rivian shows "rings around it [the car] warn of nearby objects" at low speed — [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/reviews/735802/rivian-r1s-infotainment-review-2024/)
- Path colored by control state: FrogPilot path edges green (default), orange (Experimental), blue (navigation), light blue (Always-On Lateral), red (Traffic Mode), yellow (conditional override); "Dynamic Path Width" shrinks/grows with engagement — [FrogPilot wiki](https://frogpilot.wiki.gg/wiki/Visuals); sunnypilot route path blue -> green when navigate-on-openpilot is active (0.9.4 upstream) — [comma blog](https://blog.comma.ai/094release/); sunnypilot draws a "Steering Arc" for the lateral target — [sunnypilot Visuals](https://community.sunnypilot.ai/t/3690)
- Lane-line geometry: FrogPilot's default lane-line width "matches the 4-inch MUTCD average" and road edges default to half that — [FrogPilot wiki](https://frogpilot.wiki.gg/wiki/Visuals)
- Lead vehicle: chevron with optional distance/speed/time below it (sunnypilot "Display Metrics Below Chevron"); FrogPilot allows hiding the lead marker — [sunnypilot Visuals](https://community.sunnypilot.ai/t/3690); [FrogPilot wiki](https://frogpilot.wiki.gg/wiki/Visuals)
- Hands-free availability cue: Rivian's "flashing hands appear around the Highway Assist icon" — [RivianTrackr](https://riviantrackr.com/2025-06/)
- Speed-limit escalation: Tesla's detected sign "enlarges when you exceed it", with Off / Display / Chime warning levels — [Model 3 manual mirror](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1372306/Tesla-3-2018.html?page=80)
- Alert ladder with sound reserved for upper tiers: openpilot small/normal (soft pre-alert or none) -> mid/userPrompt (prompt chime) -> full/critical (warningImmediate); engagement/disengagement are chime-only with no text — [events.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/selfdrive/selfdrived/events.py); Euro NCAP requires microsleep/sleep warnings to be "distinct from and deliver a higher perceived level of urgency than" distraction warnings — [Euro NCAP DE v1.2](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- Alerts must not be occluded by other panels: Rivian fixed "Take control of the vehicle" being "blocked by the camera view or another panel" — [RivianTrackr](https://riviantrackr.com/2025-06/)
- Information hierarchy: essential driving information (speed, limit, ADAS status, lights) in the 30-degree line-of-sight cone; a fixed direct-touch bar; at most 2 menu steps — [Euro NCAP DE v1.2](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf); Mercedes keeps the map always visible and surfaces the rest as suggestions — [carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/mercedes-benz-c-class-and-s-class-get-ai-multimedia-tech-139538); Hyundai shows a minimalist range/battery/map/weather/media "screensaver" by default — [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/reviews/722560/hyundai-ioniq-5-tech-review/)
- Night/dark modes: Lucid added Auto Dark Mode for navigation (UX 3.0) and a brightness slider in Quick Controls — [Electrek](https://electrek.co/?p=486096); [Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/lucid-airs-user-interface-has-four-screens-some-button-1846975789); Hyundai offers light or dark backgrounds — [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/reviews/722560/hyundai-ioniq-5-tech-review/); FrogPilot "Standby Mode" and sunnypilot "Screen Off while driving" turn the screen off onroad and wake "for engagement changes or important alerts" — [FrogPilot wiki](https://frogpilot.wiki.gg/wiki/Visuals); [sunnypilot changelog](https://community.sunnypilot.ai/t/changelog-sunnypilot/363); NHTSA requires dynamic non-safety information to be dimmable or blankable while driving — [NHTSA Guidelines](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Degraded-sensor disclosure: Euro NCAP requires a visual notice within 10 s when driver monitoring is non-functional, persisting or repeating every 10 minutes — [Euro NCAP DE v1.2](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf); NHTSA: device status and detected malfunctions likely to affect safety should be shown — [NHTSA Guidelines, sec. V.N](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/pdf/2013-09883.pdf)
- Color redundancy: critical color coding "should be redundant with other coding (e.g., shape coding)" — [FHWA 98-057](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/98057/ch03a.cfm); tell-tale colors follow ISO 2575 (red/yellow/green status semantics) — [ISO 2575:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/68409.html)

### Inferences
- A defensible default for this app: engaged/disengaged state as path color plus a border, lead and adjacent objects as simple shapes with a white-to-red proximity ramp, the openpilot three-tier alert ladder mapped to size + color + sound, and a thin status strip (speed, limit, ADAS state, light state) along the edge nearest the driver, with everything else collapsible.
- Confidence display should follow the research: show what is detected and which lane the car is in; avoid drawing predicted paths for other agents.
- A night profile that dims to near-black, keeps the status strip, and wakes the scene on alerts mirrors both the forks and NHTSA's dimming/blanking rule.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives numeric contrast or luminance targets for night mode on a center display (ISO 15008 clause values unavailable).
- No source describes how production cars render low-confidence detections (e.g. fading or dashed objects); only openpilot's "Hide Lead Marker" and "Dynamic Path Width" touch on it.
