## Key question 1: What is the Pulse board, who makes it, what does its own app do, and how does the Ocean's screen model affect apps?

### Takeaway
"Pulse" is OceanLink Pulse: a white-labelled EV Play (Amprocity) Android box, customised, sold and supported by ReTechImagine (CEO Majd Srour) with the Fisker Owners Association, that taps the ICC's display link and two CAN buses to add wireless CarPlay/Android Auto, a full Android app environment, cloud remote commands and ABRP, at $399 (US) / €544 (EU). Its own UI is an Android launcher that shares the screen with the Fisker UI (switching model inherited from the Rivian EV Play unit); no public spec sheet, screen list or owner review of the Pulse launcher was found, and the only first-hand description of its CAN side is this repository's own `android/README.md`.

**First-hand facts in this repository (local source, not web)**
- Pulse is "the add-on SBC the app runs on"; it "has two MCP251x CAN controllers wired to the car: `can1` = IBUS1, `can2` = IBUS2, both 500 kbit/s"; "The gateway broadcasts the body, climate, powertrain and battery status there all the time, and mirrors the ADAS bus onto IBUS2 for the head unit." Pulse "runs the Wi-Fi hotspot" the comma joins; the head unit is rooted (ARP table is read "through one long-lived `su` shell: grant it once on the rooted head unit"). An app "can open CAN sockets here, the same way Pulse's own app does" without root. "Pulse's CAN driver (an out-of-tree mcp251x) programs the chips' hardware acceptance filters" whose defaults lose seat/mirror positions, liftgate, steering angle, battery current, charging times and odometer; the filter files belong to root. "Pulse drops a large share of received frames (about half on IBUS1, two-thirds on IBUS2): both CAN controllers share one SPI bus and every interrupt lands on CPU 0." — `/Users/hiremanirmss/code/projects/fisker/oceanscape/android/README.md` lines 3-14 and 102-154
- Fork commit d5b1de0 (30 Oct 2026): "Car controls: release each event request, as Pulse's own app does", i.e. Pulse's own app sends the head unit's event-style control messages and releases them. — local `git log`, mirrored at [benkayuk/oceanscape commits](https://github.com/benkayuk/oceanscape/commits/master)

### Inferences
- Pulse injects its picture over the ICC's HSD display link (the install taps "HSD cables" and "cable #28 on the ICC connector") rather than running inside the Fisker UI; the Rivian EV Play base switches whole-screen between native UI and Android with touch passed through, so on the Ocean the HUD app competes for the same full-screen surface as Pulse's launcher, which is why this fork runs "beside Pulse's app" with its own package and port. Unverified: no document names the switching control on the Ocean.
- Pulse's launcher occupies the portrait screen while driving (Hollywood/landscape is a parked mode even in OV Loop's own software), so a HUD on Pulse is a portrait-first design; the fork's design tests at 1080×1920 (CAR_CONTROLS_PLAN.md line 78) agree.
- The owner-facing value Pulse sells is CarPlay/AA, streaming, cloud remote commands and SoC/charging telemetry; nothing in the vendor material shows a driving view, ADAS visualisation or a 3D car, so the HUD fills a gap Pulse's own app does not claim.

### Gaps
- No public spec sheet for Pulse (SoC, RAM, Android version, modem, whether it hosts a hotspot for phones). The repo says it runs the hotspot the comma joins, but the vendor site does not mention a hotspot.
- No screenshots or screen-by-screen description of Pulse's launcher/app, its automations editor, or its update mechanism were found; "Videos coming soon" is still the state of the remote-commands demo.
- No owner reviews or complaints about Pulse itself: Reddit blocks the crawler, oceanforums is paywalled, Tidal Wave shows no reviews, and the shop is "Sold out" with no review widget. The only complaint-adjacent material is the vendor's own delay notices (modem power issue, memory shortage, CE pending).
- Whether Pulse's app is root-dependent, and which control on the Ocean switches between the Fisker UI and Pulse, is undocumented.

## Key question 3: What do Fisker Ocean owners want from the comma integration, and what do they say about the stock software after the bankruptcy?

### Takeaway
The public Fisker Ocean port (jos-technion/opendbc-sunnypilot, `fisker-sunnypilot` branch, Majd Srour, Sept 2026) is lateral by default with a MADS toggle on the steering-wheel button, alpha longitudinal gated behind a panda safety flag, SecOC-stamped steering/accel frames, and `radarUnavailable = True` because the ADAS object list carries no relative velocity; press in July 2026 shows steering-only demos and owners waiting on ACC. On the stock side, owners lost cloud, OTA, Hollywood Mode and connected nav after June 2024; the paid OV Loop path sells dark mode, range reset, tethering, cabled CarPlay via a Carlinkit dongle, voice wake word and landscape mode as $299-$499 add-ons on top of $499/year, while the FOA's free Marlin bundle (VCU24, PDU400, ADAS 3.1.1) is installed in person because the 12 V battery makes OTA unsafe.

### Cited Findings

**The port itself (primary source: code)**
- Repo description "opendbc + Fisker Ocean port on sunnypilot base"; branch `fisker-sunnypilot`; `opendbc/car/fisker/` holds `carcontroller.py`, `carstate.py`, `fiskercan.py`, `interface.py`, `secoc.py`, `values.py`, `fingerprints.py`, `tests`. - `interface.py`: `ret.alphaLongitudinalAvailable = True`; with alpha_long `ret.openpilotLongitudinalControl = True` and the `LONG_CONTROL` safety flag, `longitudinalActuatorDelay = 0.30`; `ret.minSteerSpeed = 0.0`; comment "No usable radar. The ADAS module publishes a fused object list on ADASBUS ... but NO relative velocity — and the raw radar tracks (with velocity) are on a private radar<->ADAS SecOC link we don't tap. openpilot needs dRel+vRel, so longitudinal uses vision-based lead detection (like Tesla), not radar." `ret.radarUnavailable = True`; "Lateral-only by default ... the fisker panda safety mode without the LONG flag has no accel (0x121) in its TX allow-list, so the firmware physically blocks longitudinal"; `ret.dashcamOnly = False`. — same repo, `opendbc/car/fisker/interface.py`

## Key question 4: What companion-screen layouts, widgets and glance information are common and well reviewed in other EV aftermarket projects?

### Takeaway
The best-documented aftermarket companion screen, Enhance Auto's S3XY Dash for Tesla, is built on a widget grid (1x1, 1x2, 2x1 stat tiles, 2x2-to-6x2 CarPlay tile, a "Combine" tile), multiple pages that auto-switch on Drive/Autopilot/charging state, one-tap "actions" and a light theme for windshield glare; TeslaMate's 23 Grafana dashboards show what owners analyse off-car (drives, charges, efficiency, battery health, vampire drain); sunnypilot and FrogPilot's onroad options show what driving-assist users toggle (blind-spot path, lead metrics under the chevron, road name, speed limit, developer metrics, acceleration bar, standstill timer). Most of these are vendor or project documentation; independent reviews with usage rankings do not exist for any of them.

### Cited Findings

**S3XY Dash / Commander (Enhance Auto, Tesla Model 3/Y)**
- 9.6-inch touchscreen, pairs with the Gen2 Commander (OBD dongle) over Bluetooth. Placement rationale: behind the windshield "reduces eye movement from the road to the screen" and is "almost like having a Heads-Up Display"; behind the wheel for "a more traditional instrument cluster" and for people who "frequently like to tweak and readjust the layouts", at the cost of partly blocking the vents. Stats: "Speed Gauge, Battery Temperature, Remaining Capacity, Regen Levels, Blind Spot Indication, Battery Degradation Levels, AC and DC kWh charged, Current Charging Speed, Arrival Time, Estimated Battery Percentage At Destination" and more; "The stats appear on the screen as widgets, which you can customize and arrange". Multiple screens, "one default screen with statistics and controls that you use when driving, but when you enable Autopilot or you plug the car in to charge, the screen changes"; "Combine Widget, which shows various kinds of data in one place"; formats "1x1, 2x1, and 1x2"; CarPlay/AA "compact as a 2x2 widget, scale them all the way to a full-screen layout". — [enhauto blog: S3XY Dash](https://www.enhauto.com/blogs/all/the-world-s-most-advanced-tesla-dashboard) (page downloaded 2026-11-01)
- Tutorial: press-and-hold opens Page Edit to "add, duplicate, or remove pages"; stat widgets "a 1x1 square, a 1x2 vertical bar, or a 2x1 horizontal bar"; CarPlay-type widgets "from a 2x2 square to a full-screen 6x2 layout"; actions (regen, seat positioning, battery preheating) run by tap or by the right scroll wheel, with "the line underneath its name" showing state; automation conditions "drive state, acceleration mode, charging status, Autopilot on/off" ("every time you shift into Drive, the screen will automatically jump to page 2"); a Light Theme was added for "reflections and glare from the windshield at night"; audio routing lets Waze chimes go to the Dash speaker and music to the car. Limitation: the Commander "cannot maintain two connections at once". — [enhauto tutorial](https://www.enhauto.com/blogs/all/s3xy-dash-tutorial-instrument-cluster-tesla)
- Vendor product pages show roughly 28-29 reviews, about 79 % top rating; no independent long-term review surfaced (snippets). — [enhauto Commander Gen2](https://enhauto.com/products/commander-gen2-s); [Drive Tesla Canada launch note](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/enhance-auto-introduces-the-s3xy-dash-a-fully-customizable-display-with-apple-carplay-android-auto-deep-vehicle-data-deal/)

**TeslaMate (off-car Grafana dashboards)**

### Inferences
- The recurring layout pattern across S3XY Dash, TeslaMate's driving dashboards and the openpilot forks is: a small fixed grid of glanceable tiles (speed, SoC/range, power or regen, blind spot, lead distance), page sets that change by state (Drive versus Park/charging versus engaged), and one-tap actions whose current state is shown on the tile itself. The HUD already has the state-driven view idea (drive card, parking) but not a tile grid or user-arranged pages.
- Glare and night legibility are a named problem on a windshield-mounted display (S3XY light theme); the Ocean's portrait screen is lower and dimmable, but the HUD's dark/light pairs in DESIGN.md are in line with what reviewers asked for.
- Charging-state pages (current kW, arrival SoC, projected range, charge curve) are the most common "parked" content across S3XY Dash and TeslaMate, which is also where the Ocean's own Hollywood/landscape mode sits.

### Gaps
- "CANdrive" in the brief did not resolve to an EV companion-screen project; the only product by that name is Enovation Controls' J1939 gauge interface for diesel engines. — [search result](https://enovationcontrols.com/?p=4222)
- "Comma Scenes" and "AAIdea" returned nothing; AAIdea may be a misremembered name for AA Torque or dashOBD.
- No independent review with usage data exists for S3XY Dash (only vendor pages and launch coverage), TeslaMate (only one blog opinion), or Torque Pro layouts.
- Tesla Commander's full action list and the S3XY Dash's full widget catalogue ("dozens of options") are not published; only the examples quoted above.
